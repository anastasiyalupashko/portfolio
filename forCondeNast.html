About

Research built on what people actually do.

Three case studies from six years of mixed-methods research. Each one started with a question the data could see but not explain.

Longitudinal Diary Study + IDIs

How trust builds with a new product — and where it quietly breaks down

Multi-wave Same cohort tracked across months
+24% Journey completion after insight-led changes

Standard usability testing told us where people stopped in a new product onboarding flow. It did not tell us why their confidence was eroding in the weeks between sessions. The question the business needed answering was not about a moment in a task. It was about how trust with a new product develops over time — and what causes the relationships that look healthy after onboarding to quietly break down before they become habits.

Designed and ran a longitudinal programme tracking the same cohort across multiple months through diary studies and repeat in-depth interviews, mapping how confidence and trust developed or deteriorated over time. Cross-referenced diary entries with behavioural analytics to identify the moments where the lived experience diverged from what the platform data suggested was happening.

The programme surfaced an accumulation effect that cross-sectional methods had been missing entirely: not any single friction point, but a pattern of small, unresolved uncertainties that compounded over time and eroded trust before the behaviour that predicts retention had formed. Findings directly changed product investment priorities and contributed to a 24% improvement in journey completion. The methodology became a standing part of the research calendar.

Survey + Segmentation + Qual Follow-up

Reaching the audiences standard research methods could not find

+5% Conversion improvement driven by insight
3+ Distinct barrier profiles identified

The audiences the business most needed to understand — those who had signed up but never converted, or who engaged briefly and then went quiet — were invisible to standard research panels. The funnel data described their behaviour. Research needed to explain it, and the people who could explain it were the hardest to recruit through conventional channels.

Designed a primary survey programme from scratch using alternative recruitment channels built specifically to reach disengaged and hard-to-find segments. Adapted question design for low-engagement respondents, ran segmentation analysis to surface distinct barrier profiles, and followed up with qualitative interviews to explain the patterns the survey had revealed. Linked findings back to funnel and cohort data to test whether the barriers identified in research matched observed drop-off patterns.

Three distinct barrier profiles emerged, each requiring a different product and communications response. Changes driven by the insight contributed to a 5% improvement in conversion and the survey programme became a standing element of the quarterly research calendar, running continuously rather than as a one-off study.

Concept and Usability Testing

Catching the gaps before development began

Multiple Features redirected before costly build
Multi-market Studies across UK, EU and APAC

Product teams regularly brought ideas to research late, once designs were already taking shape. The goal was to move upstream: to build a research practice that sat at the front of the product development process rather than providing a check at the end of it, and that reached multiple markets simultaneously rather than validating decisions in one geography and assuming they translated to others.

Built and ran a concept and prototype testing programme combining moderated usability sessions, unmoderated prototype testing and structured concept evaluation, often running multiple studies in parallel across different audience segments and markets. Worked closely with designers to frame the right research questions early, briefed and managed external agencies for larger-scale studies and built lightweight self-serve frameworks for teams running their own tactical research.

On several occasions, early testing surfaced fundamental mismatches between a proposed feature and what audiences actually needed. Decisions that would have required significant rework after launch were caught and redirected before development began. The approach also produced the first cross-market comparison data the product team had ever had, changing how localisation decisions were made.
