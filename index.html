Onboarding, Verification & Funding Research Programme

Challenge

A new regulated trading platform was losing applicants at scale. The product team believed drop-off was concentrated at the identity verification step and had invested in optimising it. Applications were still being abandoned, and the team lacked evidence from the segments most likely to convert: users who had started and stopped, and audiences who were not completing at all.

Approach

Designed a survey programme targeting users who had abandoned the onboarding journey, including segments that standard recruitment methods could not reach. Ran structured usability testing sessions across the complete onboarding, identity verification and account funding journeys, covering multiple user scenarios and experience levels. Combined survey findings, session recordings from ContentSquare, analytics funnels from Mixpanel, and usability synthesis into a single evidence base to build a complete picture of where, why and for whom the journey was breaking down.

Key insight

Drop-off was not primarily at the verification step. Users were losing confidence earlier, at the account funding stage, where the language around margin requirements and risk warnings felt financially threatening and ambiguous. The product team had been optimising the wrong stage of the journey for months. Verification itself was completing at a higher rate than assumed; it was being reached by fewer users than it should have been.

Impact

Redirected product and content effort to the funding flow. Recommendations led to a redesign of the funding experience, risk language and onboarding communications for key segments.

Stratos Markets / Tradu  ·  2024 to 2026

Experimentation Programme

Challenge

Product decisions at Tradu were driven by opinion and seniority rather than evidence. There was no systematic link between research insight and measurable product change. Findings were shared but rarely actioned, and the business had no mechanism for testing whether its hypotheses were right before committing to them.

Approach

Built an end-to-end experimentation programme fed continuously by qualitative and quantitative research. Developed hypotheses directly from research findings, designed A/B and multivariant tests to validate them, and closed the loop by feeding test outcomes back into the research agenda. Research became the engine of the experimentation cycle rather than a separate stream running alongside it.

Key insight

The biggest conversion barriers were not where the business expected them. Homepage messaging was creating uncertainty rather than confidence in prospective traders, and primary CTAs were positioned where users' attention had already moved on.

Voice of Customer Programme: Multi-Channel Intelligence System

Challenge

At Tradu, customer feedback was fragmented across silos. Sales teams had anecdotal signals; product managers had their own intuitions; web analytics told a behavioural story with no context behind it; usability sessions generated findings that rarely left the research team. There was no unified, continuous view of the customer, and no single team could see the full picture.

Approach

Built a multi-channel VoC programme from nothing. Designed and integrated NPS surveys, in-product feedback mechanisms, structured user interviews, session analytics, usability research, and systematic input from sales calls and product manager observations into a single coherent programme with a shared taxonomy. Created a reporting cadence that gave product, commercial, marketing and senior leadership different cuts of the same evidence base, tailored to each team's decisions rather than a generic quarterly report. Research became infrastructure rather than a service teams requested when they remembered to.

Impact

Customer insight moved from the margins of planning to the centre of it. Research shaped roadmap priorities, campaign design, commercial strategy and executive decisions. Multiple product changes were directly attributable to VoC findings that would otherwise have remained invisible in separate silos.

Scaling the Research Function: From Solo Practitioner to Strategic Lead

Challenge

As research demand grew and the function's remit expanded to executive level, it became clear that a single researcher could not cover both strategic leadership and hands-on delivery. The challenge was to expand the team without losing research quality, build researcher capability deliberately, and make the transition from doing the work to leading it.

Approach

Hired and trained two researchers from the ground up. Designed their development deliberately: starting with structured methods training and supervised study execution, moving toward independent project ownership with coaching rather than oversight. Set clear expectations for research quality and rigour, gave regular feedback on their work, and took responsibility for the standard of their outputs. As their capability grew, shifted personal focus to research strategy, programme design, executive influence and the connection between research and business decisions at VP level.

Outcome

Both researchers reached independent operation on complex studies covering multiple methods. Research sat at VP level with direct influence on roadmap and commercial decisions. The team could run parallel research streams, cover more of the product and more of the customer journey, and maintain the rigour that had earned the function its credibility.

Opal AI: Research Intelligence Platform

Challenge

Qualitative synthesis was the rate-limiting step in research delivery. A growing volume of interviews, usability sessions and survey open-text responses was taking significant time to process, which delayed insight and limited how much the team could tackle within any given sprint or planning cycle. The bottleneck was not capacity. It was the manual overhead of finding patterns across large, unstructured data sets.

Approach

Built Opal AI, a purpose-designed research assistant, to automate qualitative synthesis and pattern recognition across research data. Designed it with research rigour as a non-negotiable constraint: outputs are framed as hypotheses for the researcher to validate, not findings to be accepted uncritically. Integrated it into the team's regular workflow and trained both researchers to use it as a force multiplier rather than a replacement for judgement.

Impact

Faster synthesis cycles across all qualitative streams. The team could handle broader, more complex analyses within the same timeframes: multiple simultaneous studies, larger participant sets, richer cross-stream synthesis. Opal AI became shared infrastructure for the research function and a model for how AI tooling should be introduced to research work, with clear scope, clear limits and human oversight at every output stage.
