Selected Work

Research programmes and what they produced

Qual-Led Longitudinal Study

How people really experience a new product over time

Exec Level presentation; changed product investment priorities
Multi-wave Repeat interviews tracking the same cohort over months

The brief was to understand why users who signed up for a complex financial platform were not completing the onboarding journey. Standard usability testing gave partial answers. The real question was not what broke in the moment, but how confidence and understanding developed, or failed to develop, over time.

Designed and ran a longitudinal programme combining repeat in-depth interviews, diary studies and behavioural analytics, tracking the same cohort across multiple months. Surfaced a pattern of gradual disengagement that cross-sectional methods had been missing entirely: the problem was not a single friction point but an accumulation of small uncertainties that eroded trust.

Findings were presented at executive level and directly redirected product investment away from surface-level fixes toward the trust and onboarding architecture underneath them.

Segmentation Survey Programme

Reaching the audiences standard surveys could not find

+5% Conversion improvement driven by insight-led roadmap changes
3+ Distinct barrier profiles identified across disengaged segments

Standard research panels kept returning responses from the most engaged users. The customers the business most needed to understand, those who had signed up but never converted, or who had dropped off silently, were invisible to conventional sampling approaches.

Designed a targeted survey programme from scratch using alternative recruitment channels specifically selected to reach disengaged segments. Adapted question design for low-engagement respondents, ran the analysis to identify distinct barrier profiles and followed quantitative findings with qualitative depth interviews to explain the patterns the data surfaced.

The combined insight changed how three separate product features were prioritised and contributed to a 5% improvement in conversion rate. The programme became a standing component of the quarterly research calendar.

Concept & Prototype Testing

Testing new product propositions before they were built

Multiple Product decisions redirected before costly development began
Multi-market Studies run across UK, EU and APAC audiences

Product and marketing teams regularly brought propositions to research late, when development was already underway. The challenge was establishing a research rhythm that caught problems earlier, when changes were still cheap.

Built and ran a concept and prototype testing practice that sat at the front end of the product development process. Combined moderated usability sessions, unmoderated prototype testing and structured concept evaluation across diverse audience segments in multiple markets.

On several occasions, early testing surfaced fundamental mismatches between the proposition and audience expectations that, if unaddressed, would have required significant rework after launch. The practice became the standard gate before any new feature entered development.
