Survey Measurement Programme + Segmentation Analysis

The customers generating the highest contact volumes and clearest signs of disengagement were also the least likely to respond to standard surveys. Existing insight described the experience of engaged customers well. It had almost nothing to say about the customers whose experience was failing.

Designed a VoC measurement programme from scratch using lighter-touch question design and alternative recruitment approaches to reach disengaged segments. Ran segmentation analysis to surface distinct friction profiles, followed up with qualitative interviews to understand the patterns the survey revealed, and linked findings back to contact volume and churn data to validate that the barriers in research matched observed behaviour. Reported results with explicit caveats on sampling and confidence at every stage.

Three distinct friction profiles emerged, each driven by different root causes and each requiring a different product and service response. The programme contributed to a 5% conversion improvement and became a standing element of the quarterly insight calendar. Product and customer-facing teams used the same segmentation framework to align on the customer view across functions.

Longitudinal Mixed-Methods Research
Understanding how trust and confidence develop over time with a complex product
+24% Journey completion
after insight-led changes
Multi-wave Same cohort tracked
over months

Customers were completing onboarding and showing early engagement but then quietly disengaging before the behaviours that predict long-term retention had formed. Standard session-based research could not catch something that accumulated over weeks, and the product data showed the drop-off without explaining what drove it.

Designed and ran a longitudinal programme tracking the same cohort across multiple months through diary studies and repeat in-depth interviews, mapping how confidence and trust developed or eroded over time. Cross-referenced with behavioural analytics to identify where the lived experience diverged from what the product data suggested. Synthesised qualitative and quantitative evidence into a single strategic narrative that product, marketing and customer service teams could all act on. AI tools were used to accelerate thematic analysis across diary entries, with manual verification of patterns before findings were reported.

The programme identified an accumulation effect: not a single broken moment but a pattern of small, unresolved friction that compounded across the journey and eroded confidence before retention had time to form. Changes driven by the findings contributed to a 24% improvement in journey completion.
